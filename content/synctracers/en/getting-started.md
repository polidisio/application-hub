Synctrackers reads your workouts from Apple Health, stores them on your device, syncs them with your private iCloud and exports them as JSON. It has no accounts and no server of its own.

> **Name:** on your device the app is called **Synctrackers**. "SyncSalud" is the project's internal name and appears in the code and the repository.

## Requirements

- An iPhone or iPad with iOS 17.0 or later, or a Mac with macOS 14.0 or later.
- Apple Health with workouts (from your Apple Watch or from apps like Strava or Garmin that write to Health).
- iCloud signed in, with the **same Apple ID on all your devices**, if you want to sync between them.
- English and Spanish, following the system language.

## First launch

When you open the app you'll see five swipeable pages:

1. **Welcome to Synctrackers**: *Get Started* button.
2. **Apple Health Access**: the app only reads your workouts and never modifies your data. The *Continue* button opens the iOS permission sheet.
3. **iCloud Sync**: your workouts are saved in iCloud and sync across your devices. *Got It* button.
4. **Help Improve Synctrackers**: optional. You can share anonymous usage (how often you sync, which screens you use and errors). Health data, workouts and location are never sent. Pick *Share Anonymous Usage* or *Not Now*. Change it later in Settings.
5. **Ready to Go**: *Go to Dashboard*.

Once finished, the app runs its first sync: it reads **the last year** of workouts.

## The three sections

| Tab | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Apple Health status, today's and this week's summary, streak and recent workouts. |
| **Workouts** | The full list, with search by type. |
| **Settings** | Permissions, sync, export, Vault, analytics. |

On Mac they're the same three sections, in a sidebar.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't show a workout's detail (rows don't open), doesn't let you edit or delete workouts, and has no widgets, Apple Watch app, notifications or data import. It writes nothing to Apple Health.
